# Comparisons: other ecosystems and the evidence on public money

**Question.** Which incentive designs turn public research and startup money into companies, and which produce self-perpetuating institutions?

Small regions: **Estonia** (1.36M people) 10 unicorns (~7 per million), no RTO model, "Skype mafia" alumni effect. **Catalonia** 7 unicorns, all from the software/VC scene, not its tech centres (Eurecat: 12 spin-offs in 10 years). **Denmark** 12–13 unicorns, ~2/3 moved HQ abroad. ## Cross-cutting lessons

1. **Who receives the money decides what gets optimised.** Fund the institute → institute revenue and survival. Fund the founder/company (success-contingent) or private investors with a state exit → company formation.
2. **Contract-revenue formulas penalise spin-offs by construction**, unless spin-offs and equity outcomes are weighted metrics (they are not in Emaitek 2026).
3. **Talent leaving is an output, not a leak** (ITRI, Nokia, Skype).
4. **Separate venturing from the research business**, under professional management rewarded on equity outcomes (imec; VTT → Voima Ventures).
5. **Measure outcomes, not compliance or "footprint".**

## Evidence on public vs private venture money

- **Brander, Du & Hellmann (Review of Finance 2015; NBER w16521):** 21,852 VC-backed firms, 25 countries. Moderate government VC alongside private VC → better exits; heavy government VC → worse. Outperformance only where government supports funds it doesn't wholly own. https://www.nber.org/papers/w16521
- **Grilli & Murtinu (Research Policy 2014):** government VC alone has negligible effect on sales growth; syndicates help only when the independent VC leads.
- **Cumming, Grilli & Murtinu (JCF 2017):** private-backed firms exit better than government-backed.
- **Alperovych, Hübner & Lobet (JBV 2015):** Belgian GVC-backed firms show productivity losses over 3 years.
- **Colombo, Cumming & Vismara (JTT 2016):** mixed results; design of the investment process is the key variable (privately managed matching funds worked).
- **Howell (AER 2017):** SBIR Phase I (~$150k) roughly doubles the odds of later VC; larger Phase II grants show no effect. Small, early, non-dilutive money works.
- **Lerner, *Boulevard of Broken Dreams* (2009):** government as catalyst; let the market set direction (matching requirements).
- **Shane (2009):** encouraging more start-ups is bad policy; target the few high-growth firms.
- **Mason & Brown (OECD 2014):** better start-up conditions don't produce more high-growth firms; relational/network support beats transactional support.
- **Feld, *Startup Communities* (2012), Boulder Thesis:** entrepreneurs must lead; government, universities and investors are feeders.
- **UK Business Support Simplification (2008):** 3,000+ schemes cut to <100, then 30 — a precedent for Euskadi's ~800 programmes.

**Synthesis (position, not finding):** public money should accompany private knowledge, not be the protagonist — help at the beginning, then step to the side; match private money rather than lead; its share should fall over time.
